We've come a long way! It's been more than a year since we entered the custom ROM scene. The journey has been rough. We've tried new things, made plenty of mistakes, and yes, sometimes got a little delusional.

Those failures taught us a lot. We learned, got back up, and tried again. It's still better to try than regret never trying!

Now we've reached the end of our Android 16 releases. AxionOS 2.8 Oneira is our final Android 16 update, with the September 2026 security patch. Android 17 is next, but before that, we want to share how we got here.

## What went wrong?

### Our UI inconsistencies

When AxionOS first arrived, we were trying to bring NothingOS designs to AOSP. As Android moved toward translucent designs and blur, we struggled to make that style fit with Material 3 Expressive.

We started borrowing ideas from different OEMs. You probably noticed some HelloUI influence in Personalizations. We kept looking for a sweet spot until we came back to AOSP designs for the launcher and Quick Settings. Users had to put up with a lot of changes while we figured that out.

### We kept looking for more

For the sake of uniqueness, we tried adding things that ended up hurting the user experience. We realized that too late. AxionOS became less stable, which went against one of the reasons we started it in the first place.

### Development got slow

Fixing our earlier mistakes took time. Health issues slowed us down too.

After 2.5, we also started building more of our own features. A lot of that code began as prototypes for testing. It still needed cleanup and time before we could consider it ready for daily use. That's why AxionOS stayed on the beta tag.

### What we're doing about it

We decided to drop the shenanigans and focus on getting AxionOS stable. That means letting go of some prototypes and spending more time on features that actually help people use their phones.

Folder styles have been removed in 2.8 for stability. We plan to bring them back once we figure out how to do them properly.

## Why does AxionOS exist?

It's simple. We want tech to be fun, and we want people to keep their devices for longer. That includes refurbished phones and devices their OEMs have stopped updating. Not everyone can just buy a new phone or afford a powerful one.

We build and port things that we think can make those phones smoother and more enjoyable to use. That's what we want to keep focusing on.

## So, what's new?

### Make your phone alive

Introducing AxAnimationEngine! It brings new system animations, app and page transitions, and launcher animations. We've also fixed and improved the screen-on and unlock animations.

The engine follows Material 3 Expressive motion guidelines and is integrated throughout the system.

### Twice the fun

Dual apps is here! You can now clone apps without ending up with duplicate system apps. That AOSP bug was one of the reasons we held off on enabling app cloning, even when people requested it.

### Need for speed

AxDragonite is our reworked performance framework, with a cleaner architecture and a focus on stability. If you've used the older AxionOS performance engine, the idea is familiar. This version also has some new additions from *cough* *cough* Nothing.

There's a new app optimization feature too.

We've also optimized high-quality blur. It can now render at a blur radius of up to 175 on the Snapdragon 695 with minimal hiccups.

### Make tech fun again

Quick Settings has a new look, along with a redesigned media player. You can customize the panel through the usual AOSP editor. No extra settings page, no SystemUI restarts!

You can keep just four tiles in the collapsed panel, also called QQS, and set up the full QS panel the way you like. Separate mode is also available if you want to hide QQS.

### A little more room to make it yours

The launcher gets a new Recents UI, fixes for the app drawer, and improved universal search. We've also added more control over the grid, home screen, and app drawer.

You can shake to auto-arrange icons, select multiple items in edit mode, and resize folders more freely. Folder resizing is still available even though folder styles have been removed.

Using third-party icon packs? You can now disable adaptive icons to remove the white wrapper around them. There's also a toggle for search bar history.

## Performance & system changes

There's more in this update:

* **RAM Plus:** Added swap extension.
* **AxKernelManager:** A new built-in kernel manager with more detailed controls. Developers can add more tunables, and it works with libperfmgr boosters.
* **Vulkan:** Now enforced, with an optional workaround for devices affected by media tinting. Your device maintainer needs to enable the workaround for it to take effect.
* **Memory and images:** Updated jemalloc to 5.3.1 and added jpeg-turbo performance improvements.
* **Screen recording:** More control over the recording frame rate, plus a fix for dropdown item alignment.
* **Double-tap to sleep:** Choose the status bar, lock screen, both, or turn it off.
* **AxionFX:** Various fixes and optimizations.
* **AxQuicklook:** Fixed Smartspacer issues.

## The annoying stuff we fixed

* Smart Pixels showing up in screenshots.
* Extra space left behind when hiding the gesture pill.
* Issues with the essential notifications footer.
* Video flickering when expanding the notification shade.
* Stacked power menus.
* Media player issues.
